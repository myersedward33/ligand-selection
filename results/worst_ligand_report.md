# Worst-predicted ligands

Generated by `python -m ligsel.errors`; numbers come from `results/tables/ligand_errors.csv`,
`error_by_class.csv` and `external_errors.csv`.

**Method.** Per-ligand leave-one-ligand-out predictions were recomputed for the primary elastic net
('all' view) and `baseline_cv` on the ten eligible primary datasets, with the same code and seed as
step 17. Every recomputed MCC equals `ladder_per_dataset.csv` exactly. For the external set, the
frozen model's per-ligand predictions were recomputed from `models/frozen_ni_pool.json` and reproduce
the confusion matrix in `external_test.csv` exactly; nothing was refitted or rescored.

- **Biaryls are the best-predicted class**, The elastic net's error on them (13.5%) is
  the lowest of any class and below the threshold's (21.2%).
- **Triaryls are where the threshold fails most**, it calls 74% of inactive triaryls active, because
  they sit below the steric cliff. The elastic net cuts that to 30% but misses 35% of active triaryls.
- **P-heteroatom ligands are poorly predicted by both models**, but there are only 31 predictions.

**The worst individual ligands** (`results/figures/worst_ligands.png`) are mostly small, inactive,
non-biaryl ligands with %V_bur(min) between 21 and 29, in the Ni reactions II–V. Both models call
them active, so the two models fail together rather than one fixing the other:

| P(allyl)3 | trialkyl | 22.9 | 5 of 6 | 4 of 6 | false active |
| P(i-Pr)3 | trialkyl | 29.0 | 5 of 7 | 3 of 7 | missed active (EN) |
| 1,3,5-triaza-7-phosphaadamantane (PTA) | trialkyl | 21.3 | 4 of 4 | 4 of 4 | false active |

*Hypothesis:* several of these are inactive for reasons a free-ligand DFT descriptor cannot see.
PMe3·HBF4 is a phosphonium salt (Kraken describes free PMe3), PTA carries basic amine nitrogens and is
water-soluble, P(allyl)3 has reactive alkenes, and the fluorous ligand has unusual solubility.

## 2. External test (JACS 2022 Reaction III)

11 of the 12 active ligands are Buchwald-type biaryls (`results/figures/external_actives.png`).

| Ligands | n | Frozen EN wrong | Published threshold wrong |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Buchwald-type, active | 11 | 7 | 0 |
| Buchwald-type, inactive | 10 | 0 | 1 |
| other, active | 1 | 0 | 0 |
| other, inactive | 21 | 13 | 15 |

- **The frozen model's failure is almost entirely missed Buchwald-type actives.** On the inactive
  non-biaryl ligands it is slightly better than the threshold (13 vs 15 false actives).
- All 7 missed actives sit just under the threshold, at %V_bur(min) 29.6–31.7, so the threshold finds
  every one. 3 of the 7 (PhDavePhos, RuPhos, DavePhos) are not in the Ni pool; 4 are.
- *Observed pattern, to verify:* every missed active (SPhos, XPhos, RuPhos, MePhos, DavePhos,
  PhDavePhos, cataCXium POMeCy) carries a substituent (OMe, NMe2, Me, Oi-Pr or i-Pr) on the
  non-phosphorus ring, ortho to the biaryl bond. The four biaryl actives it found (CyJohnPhos,
  MeJohnPhos, cataCXium PCy and PInCy) have none there. With 11 ligands this is a lead, not a result.
- *Hypothesis:* the per-dataset results suggest biaryls are easy within a reaction, so what fails here is
  transfer. The steric cliff, which the cliffs paper links to ligation state, carries over to a new
  reaction; whatever the elastic net learned beyond it from reactions I–V does not.
